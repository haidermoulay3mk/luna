# TEAM_MISSIONS.md · the team's own 16 Moon missions (with NASA links)

Written by Team Sol One. This is the "sheet Boss pastes" that LUNA_BUILD.md refers to. Units: 1 unit = 5 points, Time = 1 sol (see STATE.md). Wording fixes for M1, M5, M12 and M13 are in LUNA_BUILD.md, B10.

MISSION 1: FIRST LIGHT
Main idea: Limited electricity.
Situation: Your team has just arrived at the Moon base. The solar panels are working, but the batteries don't have enough power for everything.
Problem: You must decide how to use the available electricity.
Objective: Manage electricity without wasting power.
Choice A: Save power for later
Cost: Crew drops by 2 points.
Effect: Saves 3 Power.
Risk: The base gets colder because less power is used for heating.
Choice B: Use power for heating
Cost: 3 Power.
Effect: Keeps the crew warm.
Risk: Batteries drain faster.
Choice C: Use power for communication
Cost: 2 Power and 1 Crew point.
Effect: Sends information to Earth.
Risk: Heaters stay off, making the base colder.
NASA fact: Solar panels can provide electricity on the Moon. However, batteries and other power systems are needed when sunlight is unavailable.
NASA source:
https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-systems/


MISSION 2: BREATHING ROOM
Main idea: Life support needs electricity.
Situation: The crew notices that the machines providing breathable air are using a lot of electricity.
Problem: The crew needs oxygen and clean air, but the base also needs power for other equipment.
Objective: Keep the air safe while managing electricity.
Choice A: Run air machines on full
Cost: 3 Power.
Effect: Keeps life support running at full capacity.
Risk: Batteries drain faster.
Choice B: Turn air machines down
Cost: 3 Crew points.
Effect: Saves 2 Power.
Risk: Oxygen levels or carbon dioxide removal could become unsafe.
Choice C: Shut off heaters and science lab
Cost: 2 Crew points and 1 Time.
Effect: Saves 3 Power for life support.
Risk: The base becomes too cold if heating stays off.
NASA fact: NASA uses life-support systems to provide oxygen, remove carbon dioxide, and maintain safe air inside spacecraft.
NASA source:
https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/


MISSION 3: THE WATER SEARCH
Main idea: Finding water on the Moon.
Situation: The Moon base needs more water. Scientists believe frozen water may exist inside some very cold craters near the Moon's poles.
Problem: Searching for ice takes time, electricity, and equipment.
Objective: Search for water ice without wasting resources.
Choice A: Send a rover to search
Cost: 3 Power and 2 Time.
Effect: Collects information about possible ice locations.
Risk: The rover may not find usable ice.
Choice B: Search a nearby crater
Cost: 2 Time and 1 Supplies.
Effect: Investigates a possible water source.
Risk: The crater may contain little or no ice.
Choice C: Save the stored water
Cost: 1 Crew point.
Effect: Reduces water use while the crew waits for more information.
Risk: Some activities must be limited.
NASA fact: NASA has confirmed water ice in permanently shadowed regions of the Moon. Scientists are studying whether this ice could support future astronauts.
NASA source:
https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-water-and-ices/

Situation: The base is near the Moon's south pole. A nearby hill casts a long shadow over part of the solar panel area.
Problem: The panels cannot collect enough sunlight while they remain in shadow.
Objective: Find a way to collect more solar energy.
Choice A: Move panels into sunlight
Cost: 2 Time and 1 Supplies.
Effect: More sunlight reaches the panels.
Risk: Moving equipment takes time and resources.
Choice B: Raise the solar panels
Cost: 2 Supplies and 1 Power.
Effect: The panels may receive more sunlight above some nearby obstacles.
Risk: The raised structure needs extra support.
Choice C: Keep the panels where they are
Cost: 2 Power later.
Effect: Saves time and maintenance supplies now.
Risk: The panels continue collecting less energy.
NASA fact: Near the Moon's south pole, the Sun stays close to the horizon. Hills and craters can create very long shadows that affect solar power.
NASA source:
https://science.nasa.gov/resource/shadows-near-the-moons-south-pole/

MISSION 14: BLACKOUT
Main idea: Emergency electricity shortage.
Situation: The solar storm has arrived. The Moon base's batteries are running low after earlier power problems.
Problem: The base cannot power everything using its remaining electricity.
Objective: Keep important systems working during the emergency.
Time limit: 10 seconds
Default choice: A
Choice A: Turn off extra gadgets
Cost: Research activities stop.
Effect: Saves 3 Power for life support.
Risk: Some equipment cannot be used.
Choice B: Use backup batteries
Cost: 3 Backup Power.
Effect: Important systems continue operating.
Risk: Emergency energy reserves become low.
Choice C: Send an emergency message
Cost: 2 Power.
Effect: Attempts to alert Mission Control on Earth.
Risk: Less electricity remains for the base.
NASA fact: Solar storms can affect spacecraft electronics and communications. Backup power systems help keep important equipment running. In this game, the blackout happens mainly because the base has already used most of its stored electricity.
NASA source:
https://science.nasa.gov/sun/solar-storms-and-flares/
Additional NASA source about Moon base power:
https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-systems/

END OF THE GAME
After completing all 16 missions, the player's final result depends on how well they managed their resources.
Successful Mission:
The crew stays safe, the base has enough resources, and all important objectives are completed.
Partial Success:
The crew remains safe, but some missions could not be completed because of limited resources.
Emergency Outcome:
The base has very few resources remaining. The crew must stop some activities and rely on emergency supplies.
Final Message:
Living on the Moon requires teamwork, planning, and scientific thinking. Every decision has a cost, and good decisions help keep the crew safe.
